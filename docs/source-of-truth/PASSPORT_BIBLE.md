# Passport Bible

## Status

Approved foundation and current source of truth.

# Passport Foundation

## Mission

Passport helps people tell, preserve, share, and relive the meaningful experiences that shape their festival lives—including people who experience festival culture in person and people who are meaningfully connected to it from afar.

## Product Identity

Passport is a personal, living record of someone’s festival life and connection to festival culture.

It gives people a place to preserve their stories, photos, videos, voices, relationships, reflections, and shared experiences across festivals and across years.

Passport is not another generic social network. It does not depend on followers, public posting, constant activity, attendance verification, or collecting digital items to provide value.

Its value begins with one person and one memory worth keeping.

That memory may come from an experience the person attended, an experience someone shared with them, a festival they explored from afar, or an experience they dream of having someday.

## Core Product Promise

A person should be able to open Passport, create their Passport, tell the story of a meaningful experience, add the photos or videos connected to it, and preserve it inside their festival journey.

They may tell that story by:

- Speaking naturally
- Writing in their own words
- Responding to thoughtful guidance from Polaris
- Adding photos and videos
- Inviting someone they trust to share another perspective
- Returning later to continue or complete the memory

A person must be able to create a valuable Passport without collecting a stamp, attending another festival, inviting friends, building a following, or posting publicly.

# The Four Experience Categories

Every festival chapter or saved festival connection must clearly belong to one of four categories.

These categories protect the honesty of a person’s Passport and prevent explored, received, or hoped-for experiences from being presented as personal attendance.

## I Experienced

The person was physically present for the festival, performance, gathering, or meaningful festival-related experience.

This category may contain:

- Personal stories
- Photos and videos
- Voice memories
- People who shared the experience
- Artists, stages, places, and performances
- Reflections added during or after the event
- Personal Relives
- Memories shared privately by others who were also connected to the experience
- Optional stamps or collectibles

“I Experienced” describes the person’s own participation. It must not be treated as proof of attendance unless Passport later introduces a separate, clearly defined verification system.

## Shared With Me

Someone who experienced a festival or festival-related moment intentionally brought the person into it from afar.

This category may contain:

- A private shared chapter
- Photos, videos, voice messages, and stories sent by the person who attended
- Responses and reflections from the person receiving the experience
- Conversations created before, during, or after the festival
- A two-perspective Relive
- Memories that both people choose to preserve together

“Shared With Me” must always identify the experience as shared rather than personally attended.

## I Explored

The person discovered or followed a festival through publicly available or community-shared material without claiming personal attendance or a private connection to an attendee.

This category may contain:

- Saved Community Relive editions
- Publicly shared Personal Relives
- Public festival stories
- Artists, stages, performances, and places the person explored
- Personal reflections about why the experience mattered to them

“I Explored” allows festival culture to be meaningful without pretending that viewing and attending are the same experience.

## I Dream Of

The person hopes to attend, experience, or become connected to the festival in the future.

This category may contain:

- Festivals they hope to attend
- Artists or performances they want to experience
- Personal reasons the experience matters to them
- Plans, dreams, and expectations
- Public Relives or Community Relive editions saved for inspiration

“I Dream Of” represents aspiration, not attendance.

A chapter may later move from “I Dream Of” to “I Experienced” if the person attends, but the earlier dream should be preserved as part of the chapter’s history rather than silently erased.

# Core Product Principles

## Memories come first

Passport begins with the experiences people do not want to forget.

Stories, photos, videos, voices, people, places, performances, relationships, and personal meaning are the foundation of the product.

Stamps, collectibles, achievements, discovery systems, and attendance signals must never replace the memory itself.

## Personal value comes before network value

Passport must be valuable to one person before it depends on friends, followers, feeds, festival partnerships, or a large community.

If someone would not keep their Passport for themselves, social features will not save the product.

## Preserving is fundamental; sharing is optional

People must be able to preserve meaningful experiences privately.

Sharing may extend the value of a memory, but it must never be required.

The person who created or contributed material controls whether it:

- Remains private
- Is shared with selected people
- Appears publicly
- Is included in a shared chapter
- Is contributed to a Community Relive
- May be downloaded or exported where those options exist

Private memories must never silently become public content.

## Shared From Afar

Passport should help someone bring a person they love into an experience that person could not attend.

The promise of Shared From Afar is:

**Bring someone you love into an experience they couldn’t attend.**

This may matter when someone cannot attend because of health, disability, distance, finances, age, caregiving responsibilities, scheduling, safety, or another personal circumstance.

Shared From Afar is not passive broadcasting. It is a private, intentional connection between people.

### Private shared chapters

A person attending a festival may create a private shared chapter and invite a specific person to experience selected parts of the festival with them.

The attending person controls what they contribute. The invited person controls what they add in response.

A private shared chapter must not become public or enter a Community Relive without separate, clear permission from the appropriate contributors.

### Two-way participation

The person attending may share:

- Photos and videos
- Voice messages
- Written stories
- Live or near-live observations
- Questions
- Personal reflections

The person participating from afar may:

- Respond with messages, voice, photos, or reflections
- Ask questions
- React to specific moments
- Describe what the shared experience means to them
- Add context from their relationship with the attendee
- Help shape the shared story

The person participating from afar is not treated as a spectator or a substitute attendee. Their responses become part of a distinct shared experience created between both people.

### Two-perspective Relives

When both people choose to participate, Passport may help create a two-perspective Relive combining:

- What the attending person experienced
- What they chose to share
- How the person from afar received and responded to it
- What the exchange meant to both people

A two-perspective Relive must clearly preserve the difference between being physically present and participating from afar.

It must not combine both perspectives into a fabricated single voice or imply that both people attended.

Each person must control the use, visibility, attribution, editing, and removal of their own contributions within Passport.

## People retain their rights and control how their contributions are used within Passport

Contributors retain whatever ownership, privacy, publicity, copyright, and other legal rights they already hold in their contributions.

Uploading, sharing, or contributing material to Passport does not transfer ownership of that material to Passport.

It also does not give a contributor ownership of material or rights that already belong to someone else.

A person must not upload or authorize the use of material merely because they possess a copy of it. They must have the rights, authority, or permission necessary for the use they approve.

Passport receives only the permissions clearly granted by the contributor for the stated use.

Passport must not treat permission to store, display, share, edit, publish, include, export, or distribute a contribution as broader than the permission the contributor knowingly granted.

Before material is shared beyond its original private context, Passport must clearly explain:

- Who will be able to see it
- Where it will appear
- Whether the contributor will be identified
- How attribution will work
- Whether the material may be included in a Relive
- Whether the material may be included in a Community Relive
- Whether other people may download, export, or reshare it
- How the contributor can edit or request removal

Permission must be specific enough for the contributor to understand what they are agreeing to.

Permission to share something with one person does not automatically grant permission to publish it publicly or contribute it to a Community Relive.

Permission to use one contribution does not automatically grant permission to use a person’s other contributions.

### Attribution

Contributors must be able to understand and, where appropriate, choose how they are attributed.

Options may include:

- Their Passport identity
- A chosen display name
- Anonymous contribution
- No public attribution while retaining an internal contribution record

Passport must not imply that someone created, said, recorded, or experienced something they did not contribute.

Attribution does not establish ownership and must not be presented as proof that a contributor holds every legal right associated with the material.

### Editing and removal

Contributors should be able to edit or remove their contributions wherever reasonably possible.

Removing a contribution from Passport should remove it from future displays and future Relive editions controlled by Passport.

If the contribution appeared in a permanent Community Relive edition, Passport must not silently rewrite the historical edition. It may:

- Make the affected contribution unavailable
- Replace it with a visible redaction
- Record that material was removed at the contributor’s request
- Publish a corrected or updated edition when appropriate

Privacy and contributor safety take precedence over preserving uninterrupted playback.

### Limits after exporting or reposting

Passport cannot guarantee complete removal of material that another person has already:

- Downloaded
- Screen-recorded
- Exported
- Reposted
- Shared outside Passport
- Published through another platform

These limits must be explained before a person approves public sharing, downloadable distribution, or inclusion in an exportable Relive.

Passport must still remove or restrict the original material under its control when a valid removal request is made.

It must never use the limits of external removal as an excuse to weaken contributor controls inside Passport.

## Passport helps people express; it does not invent

Polaris may ask questions, organize thoughts, transcribe spoken memories, or help someone improve the clarity of their story.

It must not fabricate emotions, events, relationships, dialogue, quotes, or meaning the person did not provide.

It must not merge two people’s perspectives in a way that erases who experienced, contributed, or said what.

The finished memory must still feel like the contributor’s story—not content written about them by a machine.

## Festival chapters organize the journey

Each festival or meaningful festival-related experience may become a chapter inside a person’s Passport.

A chapter may contain:

- Written stories
- Spoken memories and transcriptions
- Photos and videos
- People who were part of the experience
- Artists, stages, places, and performances
- Reflections added during or after the event
- Personal Relives
- Two-perspective Relives
- Shared memories
- Saved Community Relive editions
- Optional stamps or collectibles

Each chapter must be identified as:

- I Experienced
- Shared With Me
- I Explored
- I Dream Of

Together, these chapters form a chronological and honest record of the person’s festival life and connection to festival culture.

## Festival chapters are living records

A personal festival chapter may continue to grow after the festival ends.

A person may:

- Add forgotten details
- Upload additional photos or videos
- Continue an unfinished story
- Correct their own account
- Add later reflections
- Invite another perspective
- Create or update a Personal Relive
- Preserve how the meaning of the experience changes over time

Changes to a living chapter must remain under the control of the people who created or contributed its material.

Passport should preserve meaningful history when possible. Important earlier reflections should not be silently overwritten when the person’s understanding changes.

## Reliving matters more than recording

Saving something is not the final outcome.

Passport succeeds when people return to a chapter, remember what the experience meant, continue an unfinished story, share it with someone important, or experience it again through a Relive.

A Passport should become more valuable as the years pass—even if its owner never attends another festival.

## Personal Relive belongs to the individual

A Personal Relive is created from someone’s own stories, photos, videos, voice, and memories.

It may take the form of a reel, short movie, timeline, or another personal retrospective.

Its first purpose is to help the person re-experience and preserve a meaningful chapter of their life.

Creating public or social content is optional and secondary.

The owner controls whether the Personal Relive remains private, is shared with selected people, or is made public.

## Community Relive preserves shared festival history

A Community Relive brings together voluntarily contributed stories, photos, videos, voices, and moments from people connected to the same festival.

It should show that one festival can hold many different experiences.

A Community Relive must not:

- Pretend to represent every attendee
- Reduce the festival to only its most popular moments
- Use arbitrary contributor cutoffs as a substitute for meaning
- Present exaggerated claims about what everyone felt
- Use private material without clear permission
- Erase the distinction between attending, receiving, exploring, and dreaming
- Hide who created or contributed material when attribution was promised
- Silently change after it has been published as a permanent edition

A Community Relive is a shared cultural record built from real perspectives—not a promotional highlight reel disguised as community memory.

## Community Relives use permanent editions

Festival chapters may continue growing, but a published Community Relive edition represents a defined version of the shared record at a particular time.

Once published, that edition must not silently change as new contributions arrive.

Additional material may be used to create:

- A later edition
- An expanded edition
- A corrected edition
- A separately named retrospective

Each edition should clearly show its identity or publication date so people know which version they watched or saved.

A person who saves a Community Relive should retain access to the edition they intentionally saved, subject to contributor privacy, safety, legal requirements, and removal rights.

When content must be removed from a published edition, Passport must use a visible redaction, access restriction, or edition notice rather than silently substituting different material.

Permanent editions preserve historical integrity. They do not eliminate contributor control.

## Passport includes people who could not attend

Festival culture can matter to someone even when they were not physically present.

A person who could not attend should still be able to create a meaningful Passport.

They may:

- Receive an experience through Shared From Afar
- Participate in a private shared chapter
- Create responses and reflections
- Experience a two-perspective Relive
- Explore public festival chapters and Community Relive editions
- Save public Relives for future viewing
- Record why a festival matters to them
- Preserve memories someone intentionally shared with them
- Mark experiences they dream of having

Passport must clearly distinguish among “Shared With Me,” “I Explored,” and “I Dream Of.”

None of these categories may be presented as “I Experienced.”

## Public Relives support social discovery

A person may discover Passport when someone shares a public Personal Relive, two-perspective Relive, or Community Relive edition through a social platform, message, link, or website.

A viewer should be able to open and watch publicly shared material without being forced to create a Passport or sign in merely to view it.

After viewing, Passport may offer clear, optional next actions such as:

- Explore the festival’s public Community Relive editions
- View other public stories from the festival
- Save the experience by creating a Passport
- Add a personal reflection
- Start an “I Explored” or “I Dream Of” chapter
- Create their own Passport
- Learn how Shared From Afar works

Passport must not interrupt the emotional experience with aggressive registration demands.

Public discovery should demonstrate Passport’s value before asking the viewer to join.

Watching publicly shared material does not grant the viewer permission to download, reuse, or republish it unless that permission is clearly provided.

## The experience must remain simple

A new user should quickly understand how to:

1. Create their Passport
2. Choose the correct experience category
3. Choose a festival or meaningful experience
4. Tell a story or respond to Polaris
5. Add photos or videos
6. Save the memory inside a festival chapter
7. Revisit, continue, relive, or share it

Someone opening a public Relive should be able to watch it without first completing this process.

Every additional step must earn its place.

## Mobile comes first

Passport is used where festival experiences happen and where people naturally keep their photos, videos, and messages.

The essential experience must work well on a phone.

The first useful experience should work through a mobile browser without requiring an app installation.

## Real behavior outranks assumptions

Product decisions must be based on what people actually do—not what we hope they will do.

Strong evidence includes whether people:

- Create a memory without being pressured
- Complete or return to an unfinished memory
- Add meaningful photos, videos, or voice
- Revisit a festival chapter after the initial session
- Continue a chapter after the festival has ended
- Build another chapter
- Return to their Passport over time

Compliments, stated interest, registrations, views, download counts, and one-time visits are weak evidence unless they lead to meaningful memory creation or return behavior.

# The Role of Stamps and Collectibles

Stamps and collectibles are an optional later supporting layer—not the foundation of Passport.

They may eventually help people:

- Mark a meaningful experience
- Discover moments, places, performances, or people
- Add visual identity to a festival chapter
- Connect a physical interaction to a digital memory
- Begin telling the story behind a real experience

A person must never need a stamp or collectible to:

- Create a Passport
- Tell a story
- Preserve a memory
- Build a festival chapter
- Participate through Shared From Afar
- Create a Personal or two-perspective Relive
- Share an experience
- Contribute to a Community Relive
- Explore public festival culture

QR claiming, NFC claiming, rarity levels, completion systems, hidden discoveries, achievements, and large collectible catalogs are later possibilities.

They should only be built when real behavior shows that they strengthen memory creation, return behavior, or meaningful participation.

They should not be built merely because collecting sounds engaging.

If stamps distract from stories, create unnecessary complexity, or turn Passport into a shallow reward system, they must be simplified, redesigned, or removed.

# Strategic Focus and Validation Order

Passport will first prove that people want a dedicated place to create, preserve, organize, and return to their festival memories.

The first product is not required to prove every part of the long-term vision at once.

Trying to validate memory creation, Personal Relive, Community Relive, Shared From Afar, public discovery, stamps, and social behavior simultaneously would produce weak evidence because Passport would not know which part created or destroyed value.

Validation must happen sequentially.

## Stage One: Memory creation and return behavior

The first product must test whether people:

- Create a meaningful festival memory
- Add a story, photo, video, or voice contribution
- Save it inside a clear festival chapter
- Understand where the memory went
- Return to the memory or chapter later
- Continue, revise, or add to the chapter
- Create another memory or chapter

Memory creation and return behavior are the first proof Passport needs.

If people do not create memories or return to them, the foundation is not working. Relives, community features, sharing systems, and collectibles must not be used to hide that failure.

## Stage Two: Personal Relive experiment

After memory creation and return behavior show credible value, Passport may run a small Personal Relive experiment.

This experiment should test whether a Relive:

- Makes an existing memory more meaningful
- Causes someone to revisit their chapter
- Helps someone notice or add missing parts of their story
- Is watched again
- Is shared voluntarily
- Strengthens the desire to continue building the Passport

The experiment should be limited enough to determine whether Relive improves the memory experience rather than merely producing a visually impressive output.

## Stage Three: Community Relive experiment

After Personal Relive demonstrates value, Passport may run a small Community Relive experiment with clear contributor consent and a defined permanent edition.

This experiment should test whether people:

- Voluntarily contribute selected material
- Understand how their material will be used
- Value seeing multiple real perspectives
- Watch or save the completed edition
- Return to their own memories after viewing it
- Trust the permission, attribution, privacy, editing, and removal controls

Community Relive must not be treated as validated merely because people watch a festival video.

The experiment must determine whether the community record creates value that ordinary festival recap videos do not.

## Stage Four: Shared From Afar experiment

After the individual memory experience and Relive concepts have been tested, Passport may run a small Shared From Afar experiment involving real pairs of people.

This experiment should test whether:

- An attendee intentionally chooses someone to bring into the experience
- Both people contribute without being pressured
- The private shared chapter feels meaningful to both people
- The person from afar feels included rather than shown what they missed
- The distinction between attending and participating from afar remains clear
- A two-perspective Relive preserves both voices honestly
- Both people return to the shared chapter after the festival

Shared From Afar must not be judged by message volume or content quantity alone.

Its value depends on whether it creates a meaningful connection that both people want to preserve.

## Later experiments

Only after the earlier stages produce evidence should Passport separately test:

- Public Relive discovery
- Account conversion after public viewing
- Optional stamps and collectibles
- Physical-to-digital interactions
- Broader social participation
- Festival or artist partnerships

Each experiment must have a clear question and must be small enough to fail without damaging the core product.

Feeds, marketplaces, advertising systems, large collectible catalogs, artificial-intelligence spectacle, complex gamification, and broad social-network features are possible expansions—not foundations.

They must not distract Passport from proving memory-first value.

# Company Principles

## Protect meaning from engagement pressure

Passport should not manipulate people into posting more, collecting more, sharing more, or returning more often merely to increase activity metrics.

Engagement is valuable only when it reflects genuine creation, preservation, connection, exploration, or reliving.

## Earn trust before pursuing growth

Passport may hold personal stories, relationships, voices, photos, videos, and private exchanges.

Trust, privacy, contributor rights, consent, contributor control, and reliability are product requirements—not legal language added later.

## Do not confuse scale with value

A small number of deeply meaningful Passports is stronger evidence than a large number of empty accounts.

The company should measure depth of use and return behavior before celebrating reach.

## Complexity must earn its place

Every feature creates design, technical, support, privacy, moderation, and maintenance costs.

A feature should be added only when it strengthens the core experience enough to justify those costs.

## Build from observed human behavior

Passport should be shaped by watching people create, preserve, revisit, share, receive, and relive real memories.

The company must distinguish between what people say sounds exciting and what they repeatedly choose to do.

## Preserve honest distinctions

Passport must not blur the difference between:

- Being present
- Receiving an experience from someone
- Exploring public festival culture
- Dreaming of a future experience

All four can be meaningful.

They are not interchangeable.

Honesty makes the Passport more valuable, not less inclusive.

# Long-Term Direction

Passport should become the chronological record of a person’s festival life and connection to festival culture:

- The festivals they experienced
- The experiences loved ones shared with them
- The festivals they explored from afar
- The experiences they dream of having
- The stories they told
- The people they met or stayed connected with
- The artists and places that mattered
- The photos, videos, messages, and voices they preserved
- The chapters they returned to
- The memories they shared
- The Relives they created or saved
- The moments they never want to lose

Over time, Passport may connect individual memories and permanent Community Relive editions into a larger, consent-based record of festival culture.

But the individual remains the center.

Passport is not built around collecting proof that someone was there.

It is built around preserving what an experience meant—whether someone lived it in person, received it from someone they love, explored it from afar, or dreamed of experiencing it someday.
