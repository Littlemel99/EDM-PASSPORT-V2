# Passport Experience Foundation

**Status:** Approved working foundation and current source of truth.

## Purpose

Passport Experience defines how people preserve, organize, revisit, share, and participate in meaningful festival experiences.

Passport begins with memories—not stamps.

Its primary job is to help people tell the stories of their festival lives through voice, writing, photos, and videos. It also helps people share those experiences with others who attended, people who could not attend, and people discovering festival culture for the first time.

A person must be able to create a valuable Passport without collecting a single stamp.

## Core Experience

The essential Passport journey is:

1. Create a personal Passport
2. Choose a festival or experience
3. Speak the story, write it, or let Polaris guide the conversation
4. Add photos or videos
5. Save the memory inside a festival chapter
6. Return later to relive, edit, or continue the story
7. Choose whether to keep it private, share it, or contribute part of it to a Community Relive

Every major feature must strengthen this journey or prove why it deserves to exist.

## Experience Principles

### Make memory creation the heart of Passport

Passport must make preserving a festival memory feel natural and worthwhile.

Users may begin with:

- A spoken story
- A written story
- A photo
- A video
- A Polaris question
- Any combination of these

A memory does not need to be complete before it can be saved. Users may return later to add details, people, media, or reflections.

### Let people tell stories naturally

Some people express themselves better by speaking than typing.

Passport should allow users to tell their stories aloud through voice recording or transcription. Polaris may ask one relevant question at a time and help organize the response.

Passport may improve clarity, but the finished memory must remain recognizably the user’s own story.

### Begin simply

A new user should understand what Passport is and how to preserve a first memory without the founder explaining it.

The first experience should offer one obvious action and avoid long forms, unexplained navigation, or unnecessary setup.

### Save first and enrich later

Festival environments are distracting, crowded, loud, and unpredictable.

A person should be able to save a quick thought, voice recording, photo, or video immediately and finish the story later.

Passport must not require a polished story before allowing the memory to be saved.

### Organize memories into festival chapters

A user’s memories should be grouped by festival and arranged chronologically across events and years.

Each festival chapter may contain:

- Stories
- Voice recordings or transcriptions
- Photos
- Videos
- People
- Places
- Performances
- Personal reflections
- Optional stamps or collectibles

Together, these chapters should form the evolving story of the user’s festival life.

### Let privacy belong to the user

Every memory may be:

- Private
- Shared with selected people
- Public
- Partially contributed to a Community Relive

Privacy must be clear when content is saved and easy to change later.

Passport must never assume that a meaningful memory should be public.

### Design for real festival conditions

Essential actions must work:

- On a phone
- With one hand
- In bright daylight or darkness
- Around crowds and movement
- With limited attention
- With weak or interrupted service

Long forms, small controls, precise typing, and confusing multi-screen processes are unacceptable during a festival.

### Make accessibility part of the foundation

Readable text, clear contrast, large touch targets, understandable language, captions, transcripts, audio descriptions, and alternatives to sound, color, QR, or NFC interactions are requirements—not optional polish.

## Primary Experience Areas

### Create My Passport

Introduces Passport’s purpose and helps someone begin their personal record with minimal friction.

The first promise is the ability to preserve, explore, and share meaningful experiences—not the promise of earning collectibles.

**Create My Passport** is the primary entry action.

The word **claim** should be reserved for optional stamps, rewards, or collectibles introduced later.

### My Festival

My Festival acts as the user’s current festival chapter.

It provides a clear place to save quick moments during the event and return later to build complete memories.

### Memory Creation

Memory Creation helps users preserve an experience by:

- Speaking naturally
- Writing their own story
- Answering Polaris questions
- Uploading photos
- Adding videos
- Combining any of these methods

Users control what is recorded, edited, saved, and shared.

### Passport

A person’s Passport shows their relationship with festival experiences across events and years.

It should feel like a personal, living keepsake—not a generic profile or social-media account.

## Passport for Everyone

A person does not need to have attended a festival to create a Passport.

Passport should honestly distinguish between different relationships with an experience:

### I Experienced

Festivals and experiences the person physically attended.

### Shared With Me

Festival experiences personally shared with the user by someone they know.

### I Explored

Community stories and Relives the user discovered and chose to save.

### I Dream Of

Festivals and experiences the user hopes to attend or explore in the future.

These categories protect the authenticity of real attendance without treating non-attendees as second-class users.

Saved Community Relives must not be presented as the user’s own memories. They are festival stories the user values, explores, and preserves.

## Shared From Afar

Passport should help someone bring a person they care about into an experience that person could not physically attend.

The guiding promise is:

**Bring someone you love into an experience they couldn’t attend.**

An attendee may invite another person into a private shared festival chapter.

The invited person may:

- Receive moments deliberately shared with them
- Watch short videos
- View photos
- Hear spoken updates and stories
- Ask questions
- Request something they would like the attendee to capture
- Send voice, video, or written reactions
- Select favorite moments
- Help choose what appears in their shared Relive

The chapter may clearly identify the relationship:

**EDC Las Vegas 2026 — Shared with me by Frank**

Passport may create a private two-perspective Relive combining what the attendee experienced with the remote person’s questions, reactions, and favorite moments.

Shared From Afar must be designed accessibly for people unable to attend because of health, disability, distance, age, caregiving, cost, or other circumstances.

It should be presented as meaningful human connection—not as a lesser substitute for attending and not as a feature that exploits anyone’s circumstances.

## Relive

Relive transforms saved festival experiences into reels or short movies that help people experience those moments again.

### Personal Relive

A user may create a personal reel or short movie from their own:

- Stories and spoken reflections
- Photos
- Videos
- Festival details
- Saved memories
- Music or event audio Passport is legally permitted to use

The result should feel like a meaningful retelling—not a generic automated slideshow.

The user controls what is included and whether the finished Relive remains private or is shared.

### Shared From Afar Relive

A Shared From Afar Relive combines moments from the attendee with questions, messages, reactions, and selections from the person sharing the experience remotely.

The result should preserve the relationship between the two people—not merely summarize the festival.

### Community Relive

Passport may create a Community Relive from stories and moments voluntarily contributed by people who attended the same festival.

A Community Relive may combine different perspectives, including:

- Performances and stages
- Festival grounds and campsites
- Art, costumes, and traditions
- Friendships and encounters
- Emotional or unexpected moments
- Arrivals, closing sets, and journeys home

Together, these contributions may tell a richer festival story than any single attendee could capture alone.

Users must explicitly choose which stories, photos, videos, or voice recordings they contribute. Private memories must never be included automatically.

Contributors must have clear controls for:

- Permission
- Attribution
- Privacy
- Removal
- How their contribution may be edited or presented

Passport should begin with small, manually curated Community Relive experiments. Automated movie creation should not be built until real users prove they want to contribute content and value the finished result.

## Living Festival Chapters and Permanent Relive Editions

A festival community chapter may continue growing as more attendees add stories, photos, videos, and perspectives.

A finished Community Relive should not silently change whenever new material arrives.

Instead, Passport may publish permanent editions such as:

- Opening Relive
- Community Relive — 2026 Edition
- Expanded Edition
- The People
- The Stages
- Camp Life
- The Journey Home

When someone saves a Relive edition, they keep that specific edition.

New contributions may appear in the growing festival chapter or support a future expanded or themed edition. They must not automatically replace the finished movie someone already saved.

Community Relives should be selected for storytelling quality, consent, variety, and coverage—not simply filled with the first arbitrary number of submissions.

## Social Discovery and Entry

A Passport Reel shared on Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, X, or another platform should provide a clear path back to Passport.

A visitor should be able to:

1. Open the public Passport page without creating an account
2. Watch the shared Relive
3. See the festival or public chapter it came from
4. Explore other public Community Relives
5. Understand Passport’s purpose
6. Decide how they want to participate

Relevant actions may include:

- **I Was There — Add My Story**
- **Create My Passport**
- **Explore More Festival Relives**
- **Save This Relive**
- **Add This Festival to I Dream Of**

An account should be required when the person saves, creates, contributes, or personalizes something—not merely to watch a public Relive.

Shared posts should preserve a visible connection to Passport through appropriate branding, festival identification, and a direct Passport link.

A shared Reel must not lead to a passive dead end. Passport should help the visitor connect what they watched to their own experiences, relationships, interests, or future journey.

## Optional Collecting

Stamps and collectibles are a possible supporting layer—not the foundation of Passport.

If users demonstrate that they value collecting, stamps may represent:

- Festivals
- Stages
- Performances
- Traditions
- Discoveries
- People
- Meaningful moments

A stamp may help organize an experience, trigger a memory, or invite someone to tell the story behind it.

Users must never need a stamp to create, preserve, relive, or share a memory.

QR, NFC, location-based discovery, rare collectibles, and completion systems remain later possibilities. They must earn their place through real user evidence.

## Festival Heart

A Festival Heart may allow someone to identify the single memory they most want to preserve from a festival.

The idea has emotional potential, but it remains an unproven hypothesis.

Festival Hearts, Heart Relics, gifting, and collection-based unlocks must not become core features until real users show that the concept improves memory creation or Relive.

## Social Boundaries

Passport may help people preserve shared experiences, contribute to festival stories, include someone from afar, and relive meaningful moments together.

It should not prioritize:

- Follower counts
- Public popularity
- Engagement manipulation
- Endless scrolling
- Content created only to attract attention

A social feature belongs only when it strengthens a real memory, relationship, contribution, discovery, or shared experience.

## Success Standard

Passport Experience succeeds when people can:

1. Understand what Passport is
2. Preserve a memory by speaking, writing, adding a photo, or adding a video
3. Find that memory inside the correct festival chapter
4. Return later to edit, continue, or relive it
5. Control who can see or use it
6. Share an experience meaningfully with someone else
7. Explore festival stories without falsely claiming attendance
8. Still value the experience after the festival ends
9. Want to preserve, share, or explore another experience

The strongest evidence is not that someone says Passport looks cool.

The strongest evidence is that people create memories without the founder guiding them, return after the festival, watch or share a Relive, contribute voluntarily to a Community Relive, meaningfully include someone from afar, and choose to use Passport again.
